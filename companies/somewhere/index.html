Somewhere

VerifiedAgencySome experience needed somewhere.com

Recruiting agency (formerly Shepherd) that places people from South Africa, Latin America and the Philippines into full-time remote roles at US companies. They run the search and the shortlist; you are then hired by the US company itself. Roles span admin and executive assistance, bookkeeping and finance, sales, marketing, design and software. It is free to candidates - they are paid by the employer.

Can a South African join?

Bottom line: Yes — South Africans can join Somewhere.

"Somewhere is completely free for candidates. If a company hires you through us, we're paid by them - never by you."

What it pays

Bottom line: Most roles pay $1,000-$2,500 a month in USD; a handful pay in Rand instead.

Most listings state pay. Across the 210 USD monthly figures on the South African roles we currently list, the typical band is $1,000-$2,500 per month (10th to 90th percentile); a further 9 roles quote R20,000-R80,000 per month. Somewhere's own South Africa page cites "around $18,000 USD per year" as a typical full-time salary. One outlier sales role quotes far higher and is likely an annual figure mislabelled as monthly - check the listing itself.

How the money reaches you

Bottom line: The US company that hires you pays you directly, not Somewhere - so the exact method depends on your specific employer.

Somewhere does not pay you - the US company that hires you does, so the rail is set per role rather than by the platform. Their model is a one-time recruiting fee to the client, after which "you join them full-time"; they separately sell an employer-of-record service to "handle payroll, benefits, and compliance" for clients who want it, so which entity actually pays you depends on the route your employer took. Where the postings we hold say anything at all they say it per role - "paid biweekly", "on-time direct deposit pay 2x/month", and in one case "paid via Payoneer in two installments". Pay is quoted in USD. Confirm the payer, the rail and the currency at offer stage.
