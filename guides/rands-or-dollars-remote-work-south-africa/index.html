Rands or Dollars: Which Platforms Pay in Which, and Why It Matters Less Than You Think

Published 6 September 2026

The assumption worth checking

Most writing about remote work from South Africa carries an unstated premise: that dollar pay is the goal and rand pay is what you settle for. It is easy to see why. Foreign-currency earnings are the whole reason this kind of work is worth pursuing, and the arithmetic in a strong dollar month is genuinely striking.

But that premise quietly turns into advice, which is to skip anything quoting rand, and the numbers on our own board do not support it.

What the board actually splits

Of the 315 listings here that state a figure, 261 quote dollars, 46 quote rand, and 8 quote another currency, mostly pounds and euros.

Rand-quoting listings are a minority, but they are not a fringe. Ruling them out on principle removes a real slice of the available work, and it removes a slice with a particular characteristic: employers who quote rand tend to be the ones who quote at all. Cherry Assistant is the clearest example on this board. It is the only source here where every single listing states its pay, and it states it in rand.

Who pays in what

Paying you in rand, directly into a South African account:

  • Globaltize. The client pays in USD, Globaltize converts, and the South African hire is paid in rand into a South African bank account.
  • Way With Words. “Our transcribers are paid monthly directly into their South African Bank Account, irrespective of who their account is with.”
  • Cherry Assistant. Rand, quoted on each listing, from their own board.

Quoting or settling in dollars, with the conversion left to you:

  • Micro1 — USD, twice a month, through Deel.
  • Toptal and Turing — USD, through their own processes.
  • Prolific, Appen, RemoExperts, italki, Contra and Somewhere all quote or settle in USD, though the method differs on each.

Malt is worth a separate mention because it fits neither column: it pays by bank transfer in euros, dollars or pounds, roughly ten days after invoicing.

Why the label is not the decision

Three reasons the currency on the advert tells you less than it seems.

A dollar figure is not what arrives. It arrives after a conversion at somebody’s rate, minus a transfer fee, and sometimes minus a payout minimum you have not reached yet. If it comes through PayPal it arrives after the FNB step, at a fee FNB caps at 1.51%. A rand figure paid into your account by EFT arrives as itself. Compare the landed amounts, not the headline numbers.

Dollar pay is not protection unless you keep it in dollars. The reason foreign-currency earnings feel like a hedge is that a weakening rand makes them worth more here. That is real. But it lasts only as long as the money stays offshore, and if you are converting every month to live on, you carry the same exposure as everyone else, with an extra conversion cost on the way.

The spread within each currency is bigger than the gap between them. On this board, dollar-quoting monthly listings run from roughly $1,000 at the low end to $2,900 at the high end, and rand-quoting ones from R3,300 to R56,000. In both cases the difference between a weak offer and a strong one in the same currency dwarfs the difference the currency itself makes. Choosing the role well matters more than choosing the currency.

When the currency genuinely does matter

It is not never. Three cases where it is the deciding factor:

  • You are saving rather than spending. If the money is going towards something priced in foreign currency, or is simply being held, dollar pay left in dollars does something rand pay cannot.
  • The rand figure was set years ago. A local rate that has not moved in three years has quietly lost value. A dollar rate that has not moved has not, in rand terms.
  • The amounts are small. Fixed per-transaction fees and payout minimums hurt small dollar balances disproportionately. A $20 minimum withdrawal plus a $1 fee is a meaningful bite out of a first month. The same work paid in rand by EFT is not.

What to do with this

  1. Do not filter listings by currency. Filter by role, then by what actually lands.
  2. For a dollar offer, ask which payment method, and price the conversion and fees before you compare it to anything.
  3. For a rand offer, ask when the rate was last reviewed and whether it is reviewed at all.
  4. Ignore anyone who tells you rand-paying online work is automatically a scam. It is a common claim in this market and it is simply false.

The mechanics of each payment method are in how money from a foreign employer actually reaches you, and the full pay distribution is in what remote work actually pays.

Common questions

Are there legitimate online jobs that pay in rands?

Yes. 46 of the listings on this board that state a figure quote it in rand, from employers and agencies paying directly into a South African bank account. We do not treat rand pay as disqualifying and we do not filter it out.

Is it better to be paid in dollars or rands?

It depends on the rate, the payment method and the fees, not on the currency label. A dollar figure loses something to conversion and transfer on the way in; a rand figure does not. Compare what lands in your account, not what is advertised.

Which platforms pay South Africans in rand?

Globaltize converts from USD and pays the South African hire in rand into a local account. Way With Words pays monthly into a South African bank account. Cherry Assistant quotes rand on the listing itself.

Does getting paid in dollars protect me from the exchange rate?

Only while the money stays in dollars. Once you convert it to spend here, you are exposed to the same rate as everyone else, plus whatever the conversion costs.
