The Online Jobs South African Students Can Actually Get

2026-09-01

Most “online jobs for students” lists are just aggregator spam

Search “online jobs for students south africa” and you get thousands of live job-board listings that assume you can work forty hours a week, plus recycled listicles that name platforms nobody checked against South Africa specifically. Neither is much use when you have lectures, exams and a submission deadline.

Everything below is different: a platform is on this list only if it explicitly accepts South Africans in its own published words, and this site already rates it “easy” to get into — no portfolio, no employer interview, and in most cases no prior experience. That is ten platforms, not fifty, and every one links through to the full breakdown with the evidence behind it.

Tutoring — set your own hours around your timetable

Cambly. The fastest approval of any tutoring platform researched here: no live interview, no demo lesson, no teaching certificate — native or near-native English is the bar. Pay is $0.17/min (about $10.20/hour), or $0.20/min on Cambly Kids, with weekly direct deposit. Their own featured-tutor page currently lists a South Africa–based tutor. Full Cambly breakdown →

Preply. You set your own rate and schedule across languages and academic subjects; most subjects need no degree or certificate. English tutors typically charge $15–25/hour on Preply’s own figures, paid via PayPal, Payoneer, Skrill or Wise. Our evidence is a real, currently-listed South African tutor’s own profile. Full Preply breakdown →

italki. Splits into credentialed “Professional Teachers” and “Community Tutors” — the community track needs no credential at all, just native/C2 fluency and an intro video. Two currently-active South Africa–based tutors are on the platform now, both setting their own USD lesson prices. Paid via Payoneer, PayPal, Alipay or Skrill, $30 minimum withdrawal. Full italki breakdown →

Superprof. Any subject, you set your own rate, and Superprof takes no commission at all — South Africa’s average tutor rate on their own published figures is R140/hour. Read the payment line before you sign up, though: Superprof states outright that “it is not possible to receive payment via the Superprof platform in South Africa” — your student pays you directly, by whatever you agree between you, with no escrow and nobody chasing a non-payment on your behalf. Agree terms before the first lesson. Full Superprof breakdown →

Task and testing work — no interview, paid per task

Testbirds. Crowdtesting for bugs and usability issues, on your own devices, on your own schedule. Their own FAQ: bug bounties typically run €1–5 depending on severity, and “if you work at a normal speed you are usually going to earn 10 Euros or more per hour.” Usability tests pay a fixed fee instead. Paid via PayPal for South Africa, €6 minimum payout. Testbirds’ own tester count names South Africa directly: nearly 13,000 registered testers. Full Testbirds breakdown →

UserTesting. Recorded usability tests — you narrate your screen and thoughts while using a website or app. No fixed schedule; you take tests as they become available for your profile, and the reward is shown before you accept it. Their participant-facing help page is deliberately vague about which countries qualify, but their separate client-facing “Supported Countries” list names South Africa directly. PayPal only, paid roughly 14 days after a test is uploaded. Full UserTesting breakdown →

Appen (CrowdGen). Short paid microtasks — data labelling, audio recording, image and search-quality work — taken from a queue rather than assigned. Pay is per posting rather than platform-wide: a current listing states $8.50/hour for South Africa, while a separate one we found earlier ran closer to $5/hour. Paid via PayPal, Payoneer, Airtm or a direct bank transfer, in their own words. Full Appen breakdown →

TranscribeMe. Transcribing audio and video from a queue you pick up yourself. No prior transcription experience required, but you do need to pass an entrance exam first. Starting rate is $15–22 per audio hour, rising to $60–70+ on specialised teams once you qualify. Paid via PayPal. Full TranscribeMe breakdown →

If you want fixed hours instead of picking up tasks

Everything above is task-based: you work when you choose, and nobody is waiting on a shift. If you’d rather have a set part-time schedule and a steadier flow of work, Cherry Assistant is the one platform on this board rated “easy” that works more like a normal part-time job — ZAR-denominated roles, no fee to apply, current listings spanning roughly R7,500 up to R38,000 a month across different roles and seniority (their own pay notes are explicit that this is a spread, not one company-wide figure). That is a real commitment against a timetable, not something to pick up between lectures. Full Cherry Assistant breakdown →

Before you sign up to any of them

  • None of the ten above charge a fee to join. If a platform asks you to pay before you can start earning, it is not one of these ten, and it is very likely the pattern in our scam checklist.
  • Read the payment line before you start working, not after. Several of these pay PayPal only. One — Superprof — does not process any payment for South Africa at all; you are trusting the student directly.
  • “Easy” means no portfolio or employer interview, not zero effort. TranscribeMe still has an entrance exam. Tutoring platforms still expect you to actually teach a lesson worth paying for.
  • Task-based pay is real but irregular. None of these publish a monthly salary because there isn’t one — you are paid per task, per lesson or per test, and how much you earn depends on how much time you put in and how many tasks are available that week.
